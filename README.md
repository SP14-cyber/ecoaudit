# ecoaudit
## Inspiration
We are sick of brands lying to us. Walk down any store aisle or scroll through any shopping app, and you are bombarded with terms like "all-natural," "eco-friendly," "consciously sourced," and "carbon-neutral." Most of it is completely unregulated marketing jargon designed to exploit people who want to buy responsibly. The advertising laws around these terms are incredibly weak, and unless you have a degree in environmental science, it is almost impossible to tell what is a real sustainable practice and what is just corporate greenwashing. We built EcoAudit to completely change this dynamic. Instead of making another polite AI chatbot that simply answers basic questions, we wanted to build an adversarial tool. We wanted to give students and consumers a way to actively audit corporate claims, dissect massive sustainability documents, and call out greenwashing using real data and established environmental frameworks.

## What it does
EcoAudit: The AI Greenwashing Whistleblower & Citizen Science Tool shifts the user from a passive question-asker into a rigorous corporate auditor. The application handles a highly specific, multi-stage analytical workflow beginning with massive document and multimodal parsing. Users can paste marketing ad copy, upload a photo of a physical product label, or drop in a massive, multi-page corporate sustainability PDF report. The application completely eliminates vibes-based grading by evaluating the text strictly against the Federal Trade Commission Green Guides and TerraChoice’s Seven Sins of Greenwashing. It strips away the marketing fluff and renders a detailed, color-coded environmental scorecard alongside a quantified Greenwashing Risk Level. If a user thinks the AI is being too harsh or missing crucial context, they can engage the interactive debate mode. EcoAudit will defend its stance using logic and regulatory data, but if the user brings real, verifiable evidence, the AI will adaptively update its grade on the fly. Once greenwashing is exposed, the app automatically transitions from an analytical engine into an advocacy tool by compiling the failures into a professional corporate call-out letter template that users can send to the brand to demand transparency.

## How we built it
We focused on build speed, data security, and deep reasoning capabilities to optimize our intense hackathon timeline. The entire frontend framework is engineered as a full Next.js App Router project utilizing React and Tailwind CSS for a clean, highly scannability-optimized user interface. We handled all multi-stage chat states smoothly client-side without needing a heavy database layer. The AI and vision engine is powered by the Anthropic Claude 3.5 Sonnet API, which we chose specifically because it excels at massive document analysis and dense text parsing. Because Claude has native vision capabilities, we completely bypassed buggy third-party OCR libraries, allowing users to upload raw photos of packaging or labels while Claude reads the text directly. The serverless backend is built using Next.js API routes deployed as Vercel Serverless Functions, ensuring that our secret Anthropic API key stays completely hidden server-side and never leaks to the client browser. To calculate the final Greenwashing Index, the app compiles individual infractions against a weighted loss function, which can be mathematically represented as:

$$\text{Greenwashing Index} = 100 - \sum_{i=1}^{n} (w_i \cdot S_i)$$

In this equation, $w_i$ represents the severity weight assigned to a specific framework violation, and $S_i$ is a binary indicator where $S_i = 1$ if the specific sin of greenwashing is detected, and $S_i = 0$ if the claim passes compliance checks.

## Challenges we faced
Our absolute biggest technical roadblock was overcoming AI Gullibility. In our earliest alpha builds, the large language model routinely fell victim to the exact corporate greenwashing we were trying to expose. For instance, if a corporate report stated that their proprietary bio-derived polymers naturally degrade over time, Claude would believe it at face value and hand out a clean passing grade. We fixed this by writing strict Negative Constraint Guardrails into the core system prompt module located in our backend logic. We instructed the backend to treat every environmental claim as guilty until proven innocent. The app programmatically bars Claude from giving a passing grade unless the text explicitly cites verifiable, independent third-party certifications like USDA Organic, Forest Stewardship Council, Fair Trade Certified, Energy Star, or Cradle to Cradle. If those formal benchmarks are absent from the document, the grade drops immediately, forcing the AI to maintain strict algorithmic skepticism.

## Accomplishments that we're proud of
We successfully built a functional, highly defensive tool that turns AI into an active weapon for consumer advocacy. We are proud that our app handles complex visual media—reading raw text off crumpled product labels—and successfully translates dense corporate compliance data into a clear, actionable game plan for everyday consumers. We managed to take an abstract, highly complex legal and ecological issue and distill it into a simple, high-utility, student-friendly web application that provides immediate civic utility.

## What we learned
We learned the exact legal loopholes corporations use to dodge advertising laws and manipulate public perception through structural phrasing. On the technical side, we mastered how to prompt an LLM to be intentionally adversarial, how to pass visual media buffers directly to Claude's vision engine without preprocessing degradation, and how to structure secure serverless API routes in Next.js to protect critical API keys.

## What's next for EcoAudit
The long-term roadmap scales EcoAudit from an interactive web prototype into a ubiquitous mobile consumer tool. We plan to integrate direct barcode scanning APIs so users can scan an item in a store aisle and instantly pull its corporate environmental history from an open-source database. We also aim to build web scrapers to dynamically ingest localized municipal waste regulations based on the user's real-time zip code, translating highly confusing local sorting laws into absolute certainty. Finally, we want to launch a crowdsourced public ledger database where all student-audited products and their corresponding framework scorecards are saved openly to establish a community-driven baseline for brand accountability.
